I am a master’s student in Information Management at the University of Washington, focusing on Data Science and Business Intelligence.

This quarter, I want to improve my ability to turn data into clear, engaging, and interactive visual stories.

For HWK 4, I focused on working contexts because I often lose track of time during long focus sessions and wanted to design clocks that make time awareness clearer, less stressful, and more engaging.

Documenting iterative process

Meeting Wave Clock: from initial sketch to refined design.

Context of use: This clock is designed for meeting-heavy workdays. It could be used as a calendar dashboard or work monitor by students, professionals, and team members who need to quickly understand their daily meeting load. I chose this context because I often need a low-stress way to recognize busy periods without reading every calendar block.

Final Clock 1 — Meeting Wave Clock

Sketch iteration documentation (paper/hand-drawn photos)

Design process: The Meeting Wave Clock evolved from a rough workload visualization into a clearer calendar-based clock. In the first sketch, time was shown as a wave across the workday, with higher peaks representing heavier meeting intensity. After feedback, I refined the design by smoothing the wave, improving the red/blue contrast, and making the 9AM–6PM timeline easier to scan.

I applied readability, hierarchy, color, layout, and motion principles. The horizontal timeline supports quick pattern recognition, the red area highlights peak meeting density, and the large current time display creates a clear visual hierarchy. These decisions make the clock practical for a working context while still making meeting pressure visually intuitive.

Time encoding: Hour is represented by the wave trend across the workday. Minute is represented by wave amplitude and meeting intensity within each time period. Second is shown through subtle motion. Peak meeting periods are highlighted in red, while normal meeting load is shown in blue.

Self-reflection / future work: In the future, I would connect this clock to real calendar data so the wave reflects actual meetings. I would also add hover details for specific time periods, so users can inspect meeting information without cluttering the main view.

I chose this context because I often lose track of time while working and wanted a playful, less stressful way to feel time passing.

Final Clock 2 — Cat & Fish Clock

Sketch iteration documentation (paper/hand-drawn photos)

Design process: The Cat & Fish Clock evolved from a playful concept into a more structured focus timer. In the first sketch, fish worked as time units and the cat gradually moved across the screen as time passed. After feedback, I made the fish layout more organized, enlarged the current time display, and simplified the visual elements so the clock would be easier to understand.

I applied layout, hierarchy, readability, motion, and metaphor. The fish grid makes the time units easier to count, the large timer keeps the current time clear, and the cat animation adds personality without overwhelming the clock. The playful metaphor helps time feel continuous and motivating instead of stressful.

Time encoding: Hour is represented as one full reset cycle. Minute is shown through the number of remaining fish. Second is represented by the cat’s movement and eating animation.

Self-reflection / future work: In the future, I would make the fish disappearance smoother so the eating transition feels more natural. I would also add a subtle label or hover interaction to explain the time mapping for first-time users.
