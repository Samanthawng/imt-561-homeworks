Documenting Iterative Process

Context of use: This clock is designed for meeting-heavy workdays. It acts as a lightweight calendar dashboard that allows users to quickly understand how busy their day is without reading individual events. This is especially useful during focused work sessions when users want awareness without distraction.

Final Clock 1 — Meeting Wave Clock

Sketch iteration documentation (paper / hand-drawn photos)

Design process: The design evolved through multiple iterations. In the initial sketch, I explored representing meetings as a continuous wave instead of separate calendar blocks to better reflect workload flow. However, the early version made it difficult to distinguish between normal and peak periods. In the second iteration, I introduced color encoding (blue for normal, red for peak) to highlight high-intensity meeting periods. I refined the wave shape and aligned the time axis more clearly with the workday timeline. In the final version, I simplified the layout and reduced visual clutter so users can quickly scan their day at a glance. These decisions were guided by readability, visual hierarchy, color contrast, and pattern recognition principles.

Time encoding:
Hour: overall wave trend across the day
Minute: wave amplitude reflects meeting intensity
Second: subtle motion indicates real-time progression
Peak: red color highlights high meeting density

Self-reflection / future work: One limitation is that the wave currently represents an abstract pattern rather than real calendar data. In future iterations, I would connect it to live calendar data. I would also add hover interaction to allow users to inspect specific time periods without cluttering the main view.

Final Clock 2 — Cat & Fish Clock

Sketch iteration documentation (paper / hand-drawn photos)

Design process: This design evolved through several iterations. In the initial sketch, I explored representing time as a sequence of fish that gradually disappear, with the cat acting as a moving indicator. While engaging, the early layout felt unstructured and slightly difficult to interpret. In the second iteration, I organized the fish into a grid layout to improve readability. I also enlarged the main time display so users can quickly understand the exact time. In the final version, I aligned the cat’s movement with the disappearance of fish and simplified visual elements. These changes improved clarity while maintaining a playful experience. The design applies hierarchy, layout, motion, and metaphor to communicate time.

Time encoding:
Hour: one full reset cycle
Minute: number of remaining fish
Second: cat movement / eating animation

Self-reflection / future work: One limitation is that the mapping between fish and time may not be immediately clear to new users. In future iterations, I would add subtle onboarding cues. I would also improve the animation so fish disappear more smoothly and the cat’s movement feels more natural.
