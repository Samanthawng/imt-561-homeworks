Meeting Wave Clock: notes and sketch documentation.

Context of use: This clock is designed for meeting-heavy workdays. It could be used as a calendar dashboard or work monitor by students, professionals, and team members who need to understand their schedule quickly. I chose this context because I often need a low-stress way to recognize busy periods without reading every meeting block.

Final Clock 1 — Meeting Wave Clock

Sketch iteration documentation (paper/hand-drawn photo)

Design process: The Meeting Wave Clock evolved from a rough workload sketch into a clearer calendar-based visualization. In the first version, I used a wave to show how meeting pressure changes across the workday. After feedback, I simplified the wave, improved the red/blue color contrast, and made the time axis easier to read.

I applied readability, hierarchy, color, layout, and motion principles. The wave supports quick pattern recognition, the red area highlights peak meeting density, and the clean horizontal layout helps users scan the day from morning to evening.

Time encoding: Hour is represented by the wave trend across the day. Minute is represented by wave amplitude and meeting intensity. Second is shown through subtle motion. Peak periods are highlighted in red to show high meeting density.

Self-reflection / future work: In the future, I would connect the clock to real calendar data so the wave reflects actual meetings. I would also add hover details for specific time periods, so users can inspect meetings without cluttering the main view.

Final Clock 2 — Cat & Fish Clock

Sketch iteration documentation (paper/hand-drawn photo)

Design process: The Cat & Fish Clock evolved from a playful sketch into a more structured focus timer. In the first sketch, time was represented as fish that disappear as the cat moves across the screen. After feedback, I made the fish layout more organized, enlarged the current time display, and simplified the visual elements to improve clarity.

I applied layout, hierarchy, readability, motion, and metaphor. The grid layout makes the fish easier to count, the large timer keeps the current time clear, and the cat animation adds personality without overwhelming the clock.

Time encoding: Hour is represented as one full reset cycle. Minute is shown through the number of remaining fish. Second is represented by the cat’s movement and eating animation.

Self-reflection / future work: In the future, I would make the fish disappearance smoother so the eating transition feels more natural. I would also add a subtle label or hover interaction to explain the time mapping for first-time users.
